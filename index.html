Knowledge Saves Lives: Cancer Awareness & Early Detection

Understanding risk factors, recognizing early warning signs, and adopting healthy habits can dramatically improve survival rates.

Watch Our Awareness Videos

Why Early Detection Matters

Cancer occurs when abnormal cells divide uncontrollably and invade nearby tissues. Detecting cancer at an early stage—before it spreads—makes treatment significantly more effective and less invasive.

Higher Survival Rates

When diagnosed at Stage 1 or Stage 2, survival rates for many common cancers (such as breast, cervical, and colorectal cancer) exceed 80–90%.

Less Aggressive Treatment

Localized tumors often require simpler procedures and fewer rounds of chemotherapy or radiation compared to advanced-stage disease.

Routine Screening

Regular medical checkups, self-examinations, and recommended screenings can spot precancerous changes even before symptoms appear.

Key Takeaway: Never ignore persistent, unexplained changes in your body. Early medical consultation turns uncertainty into timely action.

Common Types of Cancer

Cancers are named after the organ or type of cell where they originate. Here are four major categories frequently highlighted in public health awareness:

Breast Cancer

Forms in the cells of the breasts. Regular self-checks and mammograms help detect lumps or tissue changes early.

Lung Cancer

Begins in the lungs and is strongly linked to tobacco smoke and air pollutants, though non-smokers can also be affected.

Oral & Throat Cancer

Develops on the lips, tongue, gums, or throat. Closely associated with tobacco chewing, smoking, and betel nut use.

Colorectal & Skin Cancers

Colorectal cancer affects the colon or rectum, while skin cancer (such as melanoma) is primarily driven by ultraviolet (UV) exposure.

Causes & Early Warning Symptoms

While some genetic factors cannot be changed, environmental and lifestyle triggers account for a large share of preventable cancers.

Major Causes & Risk Factors

  • Tobacco & Alcohol: Leading preventable causes of lung, mouth, throat, and liver cancers.
  • UV & Radiation Exposure: Excessive unprotected sunlight or tanning beds damage skin cell DNA.
  • Infections: Viruses such as HPV (Human Papillomavirus) and Hepatitis B/C increase specific cancer risks.
  • Lifestyle & Genetics: Obesity, poor diet, physical inactivity, and inherited gene mutations.

Warning Signs to Watch For

  • Unexplained weight loss or persistent, extreme fatigue.
  • A new lump, thickening, or swelling anywhere on the body.
  • Sores, mouth ulcers, or skin moles that change shape or fail to heal.
  • Persistent cough, hoarseness, or difficulty swallowing.
  • Unusual bleeding, bruising, or lasting changes in bowel/bladder habits.

How to Reduce Your Risk

According to the World Health Organization (WHO), between 30% and 50% of all cancers are preventable through healthy daily choices and vaccination.

1. Avoid All Tobacco

Stay completely away from cigarettes, vaping, and smokeless tobacco, and avoid secondhand smoke.

2. Eat Balanced & Stay Active

Prioritize fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and legumes while limiting processed meats and sugary drinks. Aim for 30+ minutes of daily exercise.

3. Get Vaccinated

The HPV vaccine and Hepatitis B vaccine protect against viral infections that can lead to cervical and liver cancers.

4. Protect Your Skin

Use broad-spectrum sunscreen, wear protective clothing, and avoid harsh midday sun exposure.
