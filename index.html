    <!-- Section 1: Importance of Early Detection -->

    <section id="early-detection">

      <h2>Why Early Detection Matters</h2>

      <p class="section-intro">

        Cancer occurs when abnormal cells divide uncontrollably and invade nearby tissues. Detecting cancer at an early stage—before it spreads—makes treatment significantly more effective and less invasive.

      </p>

      <div class="grid">

        <div class="card">

          <h3>Higher Survival Rates</h3>

          <p>When diagnosed at Stage 1 or Stage 2, survival rates for many common cancers (such as breast, cervical, and colorectal cancer) exceed 80–90%.</p>

        </div>

        <div class="card">

          <h3>Less Aggressive Treatment</h3>

          <p>Localized tumors often require simpler procedures and fewer rounds of chemotherapy or radiation compared to advanced-stage disease.</p>

        </div>

        <div class="card">

          <h3>Routine Screening</h3>

          <p>Regular medical checkups, self-examinations, and recommended screenings can spot precancerous changes even before symptoms appear.</p>

        </div>

      </div>

      <div class="callout">

        <strong>Key Takeaway:</strong> Never ignore persistent, unexplained changes in your body. Early medical consultation turns uncertainty into timely action.

      </div>

    </section>


    <!-- Section 2: Common Types of Cancer -->

    <section id="types">

      <h2>Common Types of Cancer</h2>

      <p class="section-intro">

        Cancers are named after the organ or type of cell where they originate. Here are four major categories frequently highlighted in public health awareness:

      </p>

      <div class="grid">

        <div class="card">

          <h3>Breast Cancer</h3>

          <p>Forms in the cells of the breasts. Regular self-checks and mammograms help detect lumps or tissue changes early.</p>

        </div>

        <div class="card">

          <h3>Lung Cancer</h3>

          <p>Begins in the lungs and is strongly linked to tobacco smoke and air pollutants, though non-smokers can also be affected.</p>

        </div>

        <div class="card">

          <h3>Oral &amp; Throat Cancer</h3>

          <p>Develops on the lips, tongue, gums, or throat. Closely associated with tobacco chewing, smoking, and betel nut use.</p>

        </div>

        <div class="card">

          <h3>Colorectal &amp; Skin Cancers</h3>

          <p>Colorectal cancer affects the colon or rectum, while skin cancer (such as melanoma) is primarily driven by ultraviolet (UV) exposure.</p>

        </div>

      </div>

    </section>


    <!-- Section 3: Causes & Warning Symptoms -->

    <section id="causes-symptoms">

      <h2>Causes &amp; Early Warning Symptoms</h2>

      <p class="section-intro">

        While some genetic factors cannot be changed, environmental and lifestyle triggers account for a large share of preventable cancers.

      </p>

      <div class="grid">

        <div class="card">

          <h3>Major Causes &amp; Risk Factors</h3>

          <ul>

            <li><strong>Tobacco &amp; Alcohol:</strong> Leading preventable causes of lung, mouth, throat, and liver cancers.</li>

            <li><strong>UV &amp; Radiation Exposure:</strong> Excessive unprotected sunlight or tanning beds damage skin cell DNA.</li>

            <li><strong>Infections:</strong> Viruses such as HPV (Human Papillomavirus) and Hepatitis B/C increase specific cancer risks.</li>

            <li><strong>Lifestyle &amp; Genetics:</strong> Obesity, poor diet, physical inactivity, and inherited gene mutations.</li>

          </ul>

        </div>

        <div class="card">

          <h3>Warning Signs to Watch For</h3>

          <ul>

            <li>Unexplained weight loss or persistent, extreme fatigue.</li>

            <li>A new lump, thickening, or swelling anywhere on the body.</li>

            <li>Sores, mouth ulcers, or skin moles that change shape or fail to heal.</li>

            <li>Persistent cough, hoarseness, or difficulty swallowing.</li>

            <li>Unusual bleeding, bruising, or lasting changes in bowel/bladder habits.</li>

          </ul>

        </div>

      </div>

    </section>


    <!-- Section 4: Prevention -->

    <section id="prevention">

      <h2>How to Reduce Your Risk</h2>

      <p class="section-intro">

        According to the World Health Organization (WHO), between 30% and 50% of all cancers are preventable through healthy daily choices and vaccination.

      </p>

      <div class="grid">

        <div class="card">

          <h3>1. Avoid All Tobacco</h3>

          <p>Stay completely away from cigarettes, vaping, and smokeless tobacco, and avoid secondhand smoke.</p>

        </div>

        <div class="card">

          <h3>2. Eat Balanced &amp; Stay Active</h3>

          <p>Prioritize fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and legumes while limiting processed meats and sugary drinks. Aim for 30+ minutes of daily exercise.</p>

        </div>

        <div class="card">

          <h3>3. Get Vaccinated</h3>

          <p>The <strong>HPV vaccine</strong> and <strong>Hepatitis B vaccine</strong> protect against viral infections that can lead to cervical and liver cancers.</p>

        </div>

        <div class="card">

          <h3>4. Protect Your Skin</h3>

          <p>Use broad-spectrum sunscreen, wear protective clothing, and avoid harsh midday sun exposure.</p>

        </div>

      </div>

    </section>
